Ask anyone over 55 about their knees and you'll hear about stairs. Not marathons. Stairs. The landing at the in-laws' house. The basement steps with the laundry basket. The moment on the floor with a grandchild when getting back up takes a plan.

For years the go-to answer at the pharmacy has been the same: a big glucosamine tablet, a big chondroitin tablet, sometimes both. A common daily routine is 1,500 mg of one plus 1,200 mg of the other. That's 2,700 milligrams of pills, every day.

"The interesting thing," says [Advisor last name], "is that one of the better-studied joint ingredients works at a tiny fraction of that. It isn't meant to be raw building material at all."

1. It was never about bigger pills

That ingredient is UC-II®, an undenatured type II collagen made from chicken sternum cartilage by the ingredient company Lonza. It's studied at just 40 mg a day, about the size of one small tablet.

"Undenatured" means the collagen keeps its native, folded shape. A patented low-temperature process avoids the heat that breaks ordinary collagen powders into simple protein. That shape matters, because the theory isn't that the body uses it as a raw ingredient.

"Think of it less like a brick and more like a message."

The honest read. Several earlier trials were funded by the ingredient's maker. The improvements were real in those studies but modest and gradual, measured at 90 to 180 days. One independent trial found nothing. "That's not a reason to dismiss it," [Advisor last name] says. "It's a reason to measure it."

3. Don't take anyone's word for it. Measure it.

Because effects in the studies were gradual, the most useful thing you can do is record a baseline before your first tablet and re-test later. That's the idea behind the free Knee180 tracker kit, and it borrows simple tests anyone can do at home:

  1. Stair time. Time one flight of stairs, up and down, at a comfortable pace.
  2. 30-second chair stand. Count how many times you can stand up from a chair in 30 seconds (a test the CDC's STEADI program uses for older adults).
  3. Five everyday moves. Score stairs, standing up, a 15-minute walk, kneeling or gardening, and the first steps of the morning from 0 to 10.
  4. Re-test at Day 90 and Day 180. Compare against Day 0, on paper.
